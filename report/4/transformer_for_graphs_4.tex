\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Preliminary.} The results below rest on a \emph{single} training
seed (plus the eight reproduction seeds of Report~III for the baseline). They are
meant to fix the experimental design and the first qualitative reading; every
number will change once the multi-seed and $n=40$ runs land.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

This fourth report follows two findings from the earlier ones. The \emph{data
lever} does not help a standard transformer generalise (Report~III, Part~I:
the outcome is seed-dominated), and an $L=2$ model hits a sharp capacity wall at
shortest-path distance $\approx 3^L = 9$ (Part~II). The wall is a property of the
model, not of the training data, so steering the data cannot move it. Here we ask
whether a \emph{spectral} ingredient can, and, more basically, whether the
diameter is even the right way to measure how hard a graph is. Three threads:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Does a Laplacian / similarity read-out help?}
    (Section~\ref{sec:lap}.) Connectivity is a spectral property, so we try a
    similarity read-out ($\hat R_{ij}\propto\cos(h_i,h_j)$) and an auxiliary
    Laplacian-smoothness loss.
    \item \textbf{Is the diameter the right notion of difficulty?}
    (Section~\ref{sec:diff}.) We evaluate on a panel of graph families spanning
    the diameter$\times$spectral-gap plane and ask which axis tracks the error.
    \item \textbf{Is the spectral gap the bottleneck?} (Section~\ref{sec:gap}.)
    We sweep the gap at fixed structure to isolate it from family confounds.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model and training.} The RoBERTa-faithful standard transformer of
Report~III ($L=2$, single head, $d_{\mathrm{model}}=512$, normalised-ReLU
attention), trained online at $n=20$ for $10^6$ steps. Two training regimes:
\textbf{ER} (Set~A, $\mathrm{ER}(20,0.08)$ only, as in the paper) and
\textbf{mixed} (Set~B, a uniform mixture of nine families). Two read-outs
(\emph{linear}, \emph{similarity}) and an optional Laplacian loss
$\lambda\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\|h_i-h_j\|^2$.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Every checkpoint is tested on a panel of $11$ families and
the accuracy is broken down three ways: pairwise accuracy vs shortest-path
distance (the capacity view), and exact-match / pairwise vs graph
\textbf{diameter} and vs \textbf{spectral gap} (the smallest non-zero eigenvalue
of the normalised Laplacian). The families are chosen to spread out in the
diameter$\times$gap plane; \emph{expander} (random regular) and \emph{barbell}
(two cliques joined by a path) are deliberate opposites, and are held out of the
mixed training set so they probe unseen structure.

\section{The Laplacian loss is inert; the similarity read-out is the only knob}
\label{sec:lap}

In an earlier single-seed study at $n=64$, a large weight $\lambda=1$
\emph{collapsed} the model to the trivial ``all-connected'' predictor (the
smoothness term, $O(d_{\mathrm{model}})$ at init, swamps the $O(1)$
cross-entropy), while the similarity read-out on its own extended the model's
reach. Guided by that, here we use a small $\lambda$ with a warm-up
(off for the first $50$k steps, then ramped). The sweep
$\lambda\in\{0,10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$ at one seed gives a clean, slightly
deflating answer: \textbf{at convergence $\lambda$ is inert}. The Laplacian
energy falls to $\approx 0$ on its own under the similarity read-out --- aligning
the embeddings of connected nodes is exactly what $\cos(h_i,h_j)\!\approx\!1$
already does --- so the auxiliary term has nothing left to push, and all
$\lambda$ values reach the same in-distribution accuracy with no collapse. The
read-out, not the loss, is the lever; we drop $\lambda$ in the larger runs.

The cleanest comparison is \emph{barbell vs.\ 1chain}: the barbell has the

the single-component families the diameter orders difficulty \emph{backwards} and
the gap orders it correctly. The expander seals it: smallest diameter, largest
gap, essentially solved ($0.99$) --- and it was never seen in training.

Two families break the gap ordering --- \textbf{2chains} (gap $0.06$, exact
$0.10$) and \textbf{2cliques} (gap $1.11$, exact $0.38$). Both are multi-component
graphs the model fails to \emph{cut}, not to reach: separating components is a
different sub-task from confirming long-range connection, and the gap speaks to
the latter. This is the distinction the next round measures explicitly (we now
log reach- and cut-accuracy separately).

\section{Spectral gap: promising, but to be isolated}
\label{sec:gap}

Table~\ref{tab:fam} mixes families, so ``small gap is hard'' is still confounded
with ``which family''. Worse, the barbell does not even cleanly separate gap from
\emph{distance}: its hard pairs are also its far pairs, so its difficulty might
still be the diameter. We need an experiment that holds distance fixed.

\paragraph{The controlled probe: distance fixed, paths varied.} We build graphs
where two terminal nodes $s,t$ are joined by $k$ internally-disjoint paths, each
of length $\ell$ edges. The shortest-path distance $s\!-\!t$ is $\ell$ for
\emph{every} $k$, but the effective resistance is $\ell/k$, falling with $k$. We
vary $k$ at fixed $\ell$ and read the model's error on the $(s,t)$ pair. The two
hypotheses give opposite predictions: if difficulty is \emph{distance} (the
paper) the error is flat in $k$; if it is \emph{resistance / bottleneck} (our
reading) the error falls with $k$. We measure, on $(s,t)$, the prediction, the
embedding closeness $1-\cos(h_s,h_t)$, and the model's true end-to-end influence
$\|\partial h_t/\partial x_s\|$ (real weights), against the structural
$R_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $P^{3^L}$ and distance. The contrast is sharpest \emph{past}
the $3^L=9$ capacity, which needs room, so this runs on the $n=64$ reach
checkpoints (and $n=40$), not at $n=20$.

\paragraph{Observational sweep.} In parallel, to vary the gap continuously
\emph{at fixed family} we add an \emph{expander} of varying degree (gap
$\approx 0.05$--$0.6$) and a \emph{barbell} of varying bridge length (gap
$\approx 0.006$--$0.02$), both single-component (pure reach); the eval already
buckets accuracy by gap. Across families we rank by $P^L$ (a probability in
$[0,1]$, comparable across graphs) rather than raw resistance (whose scale
depends on the edge count).

A caveat on scale. At $n=20$ the $3^L=9$ wall of Part~II is barely visible:
the disconnected families have components too small to contain within-distances
beyond $9$ (a $2$-chain of $k=10$ tops out at $d=9$), and the long-distance
families are single-component, where ``reach'' is trivially high. Seeing the wall
needs large components, i.e.\ larger $n$; $n=40$ runs (where $2$-chains and
path-unions reach $d\approx 19$) are training now, alongside a \emph{chain-plus}
test family (a long chain plus a small separate component) that exposes
within-distances past $9$ together with a genuine disconnection.

\section{What is running}
\label{sec:status}

To turn the single-seed picture into something defensible, the following are in
flight on the cluster: (i) the $n=20$ family runs at the remaining seeds
($2$--$13$k), $\{$ER, mixed$\}\times\{$linear, similarity$\}$, for Sections

visible over a wide distance range (Section~\ref{sec:diff}/\ref{sec:gap});
(iii) a re-evaluation of every checkpoint on the full family panel with the
reach/cut split and the gap sweep. The controlled distance-vs-paths probe of
Section~\ref{sec:gap} will be run on the existing $n=64$ reach checkpoints.

\section{Takeaway}

On one seed: the Laplacian loss is redundant with the similarity read-out and is
dropped; the diameter is the \emph{wrong} difficulty axis (the barbell, short in
diameter but bottlenecked, is harder than a long chain), and the spectral gap ---
or more precisely the resistance / bottleneck the model must push information
through --- is the better candidate, modulo a separate ``cut'' sub-task for
multi-component graphs. The controlled distance-vs-paths probe, the gap sweep and
the $n=40$ wall measurement will turn these single-seed observations into
something we can stand behind.
