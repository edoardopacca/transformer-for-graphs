\section{Where this comes from}
\label{sec:intro}

Report~IX, Thread~A.3 (\texttt{report/9/transformer\_for\_graphs\_9.tex}, and the mechanistic
follow-up \texttt{stagewise\_threeway.py}) tested the split-chains-only $n{=}46$ seed-$1000$
checkpoint on every three-way partition of the canvas. Two cells stood out on inspection of the
five-stage similarity-geometry heatmaps:

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
  \item \textbf{$(15,15,16)$} --- fully balanced. Every within-component block in the final-stage
        geometry is already visibly darker/more positive than the cross-component blocks (it
        \emph{looks} like the model has separated the three components correctly), yet all three
        pairwise cuts fail and exact match is $0$: the cross-component logits sit just
        \emph{above} the model's own decision threshold, not far below it.
  \item \textbf{$(7,15,24)$} --- two of the three cuts succeed, cut$(2,3)$ (middle vs.\ largest)
        fails; the 24-node component also contains pairs beyond the doubled wall
        $2\cdot3^L{=}18$.
\end{itemize}

This document runs \emph{two} fine-tuning experiments off the same starting checkpoint, asking
two related but different questions.

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
  \item \textbf{Experiment~1 (targeted, \S\ref{sec:setup}--\S\ref{sec:verdict-a}).} Does
        fine-tuning \emph{only} the similarity read-out's two global scalars (scale, bias) ---
        everything else in the trunk frozen --- on a stream containing \emph{only these two named
        cells} fix them, without breaking the ordinary $K{=}2$ split-chains behaviour the
        checkpoint already has?
  \item \textbf{Experiment~2 (full distribution, \S\ref{sec:setup-full}--\S\ref{sec:verdict-b}).}
        Does the same two-scalar fine-tuning, given exposure to \emph{every} three-way split
        instead of just two cells, and a much longer budget, learn a genuinely \emph{general}
        three-component rule rather than overfitting the two cells above?
\end{itemize}

Both are narrower/longer than Report~IX's own fine-tuning probe
(\texttt{report/9/scratch\_prof\_experiments/scratch\_prof\_experiments.tex}, \S6), which trained
on a generic $K\in\{3,4,5,6\}$ \texttt{path\_union} stream for only $3{,}000$ steps and left
$K{\ge}3$ exact match at $0$ throughout. Fine-tuning only two scalars over a frozen trunk is
cheap, so a much longer budget costs little in either case.

\section{Experiment 1: targeted fine-tuning on the two named cells}
\label{sec:exp1}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Checkpoint.} The unmodified split-chains-only, $n{=}46$, seed-$1000$, similarity
read-out checkpoint from Report~IX
(\texttt{runs/report9/n46\_train/n46\_split\_chains\_roberta\_similarity\_lam0\_seed1000/last.pt}) ---
the same one behind every Report~IX \S A.3 three-way figure.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning.} Every parameter frozen except \texttt{sim\_scale}/\texttt{sim\_bias}
(2 scalars out of $\sim$6.3M). Training stream: uniformly, either the $(15,15,16)$ or the
$(7,15,24)$ three-way split-chain graph (\texttt{generate\_multi\_path\_split\_graph}), freshly
relabelled every sample. $50{,}000$ steps, batch $500$, AdamW lr $10^{-3}$
(\texttt{finetune\_readout\_threeway.py}).

\paragraph{What is measured.} Before fine-tuning (step $0$) and every $1{,}000$ steps after:
whole-graph exact match, per-component reach, the three pairwise cuts, and the overall
predicted-positive rate, on both target cells (so the effect on the \emph{held-out} cell is
visible even when only the other one dominates gradient updates); for $(7,15,24)$ specifically,
reach inside the 24-node component split into three shortest-path-distance buckets ---
within-capacity ($d\le9$), between-the-walls ($9<d\le18$), and beyond the doubled wall
($d>18$) --- the direct test of whether fine-tuning can push learning past the architectural
bound, not just recalibrate a threshold within it. Separately, \texttt{eval\_asym\_chains.py} on
the fine-tuned checkpoint reproduces the ordinary $K{=}2$ split-chains sweep (Report~IX
Table~3/\S sec:planA), compared against the identical, already-committed sweep on the
\emph{untouched} checkpoint (\texttt{runs/report9/asym\_chains\_n46/n46\_splitchains\_seed1000/}),
to check the two-scalar update did not cost anything in distribution.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsubsection{Does it learn the two target cells?}
\label{sec:res-curve}

Figure~\ref{fig:r10curve} answers this only partially. On $(15,15,16)$, cut$(2,3)$ climbs
cleanly from $0.20$ at step $0$ to $1.000$ by step $\sim\!10{,}000$ and stays there for the
remaining $40{,}000$ steps. Whole-graph exact match does not follow it up: it sits at $0$
throughout, with one exception --- a transient spike to $0.22$ around step $9{,}000$ that
collapses back to $0$ a thousand steps later. That spike lands exactly where cut$(2,3)$ is
still mid-climb and scale/bias are moving fastest; it is training noise at a crossing point,
not a solved cell. On $(7,15,24)$, cut$(2,3)$ only reaches a plateau around $0.50$ and exact
match never leaves $0$. Both cells show the predicted-positive rate falling sharply as
fine-tuning proceeds ($0.85\to0.27$ for $(15,15,16)$, $0.76\to0.54$ for $(7,15,24)$): the two
scalars are pushing the whole graph toward predicting fewer pairs connected overall, not
selectively toward the one pair that was wrong.

\subsubsection{Does it learn past the doubled wall?}
\label{sec:res-beyond}

Figure~\ref{fig:r10beyond} shows why the cut$(2,3)$ improvement on $(7,15,24)$ is not the win
it looks like in isolation. Before fine-tuning, reach inside the 24-node component is
$\approx1$ in all three distance buckets, including beyond the doubled wall $d>18$ ---
consistent with Report~IX's finding that reach transfers even past $2\cdot3^L$. In the same
window where cut$(2,3)$ rises (steps $\sim\!8{,}000$--$14{,}000$), reach beyond the doubled
wall collapses from $\approx1.0$ to $\approx0.10$--$0.14$ and never recovers; the
within-capacity and between-the-walls buckets take a smaller hit, settling around
$0.89$--$0.94$. The two-scalar fine-tune does not learn past the doubled wall: it trades away
almost all of the pre-existing long-range reach in the 24-node component for a partial fix to
one cross-component cut, and even that trade only buys cut$(2,3)$ up to $\approx0.50$.

\subsubsection{Does it forget the $K{=}2$ split-chains behaviour?}
\label{sec:res-ownfamily}

Figure~\ref{fig:r10ownfamily} shows the cost on the behaviour the checkpoint already had.
Before fine-tuning this is the clean step function from Report~IX
(the beyond-threshold table in \texttt{paper\_draft.tex}, \S5.1): exact match $1.000$ for every
$a\le11$, $0$ from $a=12$. After fine-tuning on $(15,15,16)$/$(7,15,24)$, the curve is worse
in two distinct ways. First, a new failure appears well inside the previously-perfect region:
$a=2$ drops to $0$ (recovering to $1.000$ again at $a=3,4$), even though $a=2$ has nothing to
do with either target cell. Second, the region $a=5$--$11$, previously flat at $1.000$, now
degrades gradually ($0.940, 0.887, 0.667, 0.567, 0.183, 0.333, 0.307$) instead of holding to
the wall. The per-split numbers behind this curve
(\texttt{runs/report10/asym\_chains\_n46/n46\_splitchains\_seed1000\_threeway\_post/asym\_chains.json})
show cut staying at exactly $1.000$ at \emph{every single split}, unchanged from before
fine-tuning: every new failure here is a reach story. At $a=2$, reach of the long component
drops to $0.190$ while reach of the short component stays $1.000$; from $a=12$ on, reach of
the long component collapses to $0.05$--$0.11$ (it was $1.000$ everywhere pre-fine-tuning).
Fine-tuning has not just cost accuracy on the $K{=}2$ family, it has flipped which of
reach/cut does the failing: the untouched checkpoint's $K{=}2$ wall was a pure cut failure
(reach $1.000$ throughout, cut collapsing from $a=12$); this checkpoint's $K{=}2$ wall is now
a reach failure with cut untouched.

\subsubsection{How much did scale/bias actually move?}
\label{sec:res-scalebias}

Before fine-tuning: scale $=31.12$, bias $=-1.38$. After the $50{,}000$-step targeted run:
scale $=11.49$ ($-63\%$), bias $=-7.87$. The pair of decision-boundary scalars moved a lot, in
both directions at once: scale shrinks toward $0$ (every cosine similarity counts for less),
and bias moves sharply negative (the model needs stronger positive evidence before declaring
two nodes connected). The post-fine-tuning bias is close to the $-5$ to $-8$ range typical of
checkpoints trained on this distribution family from scratch (this checkpoint's own
pre-fine-tuning bias, $-1.38$, was on the low/permissive end of that range); the fine-tune has
moved the decision boundary toward a stricter operating point overall, not applied a small
local correction. That fixing one specific miscalibrated cut required this large and global a
shift is itself evidence against a pure threshold-calibration story: a small nudge would have
sufficed if the two named cells' problem were only that.

\subsubsection{Mechanistic comparison: attention and the five-stage geometry, before vs.\ after}
\label{sec:res-mechanistic}

The post-fine-tuning stagewise battery
(\texttt{runs/report10/stagewise\_threeway/n46\_splitchains\_seed1000\_threeway\_post/}) reused
Report~IX's five probe cells, not just the two targets, so the untrained cells serve as a
transfer check. Since the trunk is frozen, every stage up to $H^{(2)}$ (the final read-out
geometry) is identical to Report~IX's --- only the $H^{(2)}$ decision itself can move, because
only scale/bias changed. Comparing $H^{(2)}$ cut$(1,2)$/cut$(1,3)$/cut$(2,3)$ before
(Report~IX) vs.\ after this fine-tuning:

\paragraph{Does that also hold across the whole $K{=}3$ grid, not just these five cells?}
\label{sec:res-fullgrid}
Regenerating Report~IX's full $176$-cell sweep
(\texttt{plot\_threeway\_splitchains.py}) on the fine-tuned checkpoint gives a fuller and more
surprising picture than the five named cells suggest. Figure~\ref{fig:r10fullgrid} plots
cut$(1,2)$/cut$(1,3)$/cut$(2,3)$ and exact match, averaged over every feasible $s_2$ at each
$s_1$, before vs.\ after. Before fine-tuning, cut$(1,2)$ and cut$(1,3)$ are moderate and noisy
($0.5$--$0.95$ across most of $s_1\le11$), cut$(2,3)$ is close to $0$ almost everywhere except
an isolated spike to $\approx1.0$ at $s_1{=}2$, and exact match is $0$ at every single cell.
After fine-tuning on just two cells, cut$(1,2)$ jumps to $\approx1.0$ across the \emph{entire}
range $s_1{=}1$--$15$, and cut$(1,3)$ turns from noisy into a clean rising trend from
$\approx0.65$ up to $1.0$ by $s_1{=}12$. cut$(2,3)$ stays the hardest pair --- still a jagged
function of $s_1$ with a wide dip through $s_1{\approx}6$--$9$ --- but it now reaches $1.0$
by $s_1{=}12$, where before it never left $0$.

Despite this broad, largely unintended improvement in the aggregate cut curves, whole-graph
exact match barely moves: mean exact match over the $176$ cells is $0.0100$ after fine-tuning
(a handful of cells reach as high as $0.87$, but almost all sit at $0$), essentially the same
qualitative picture as the $0$ everywhere before. Two scalars that make two of the three
pairwise cuts look substantially more solved in aggregate still cannot produce an exact match,
because (per \S\ref{sec:res-ownfamily}) the same fine-tuning is simultaneously damaging reach
--- and exact match needs every pair right at once, cut and reach together.

\subsection{Reading, once the data is in}
\label{sec:verdict-a}

(1) \textbf{Did it work on $(15,15,16)$?} Not in the sense that matters: cut$(2,3)$ reaches
$1.000$, but exact match never leaves $0$ (bar one transient noise spike) because reach on
that same cell degrades from $1.000$ to $0.73$--$0.82$ across its three components. (2)
\textbf{Did it transfer to $(7,15,24)$, or did the two cells trade off?} Partial transfer, not
a trade-off in the zero-sum sense --- both cells' cut$(2,3)$ improve simultaneously (to $1.0$
and $\approx0.50$ respectively) --- but neither reaches exact match, and $(7,15,24)$'s partial
gain costs it almost all of its long-range reach beyond the doubled wall. (3) \textbf{Did any
learning happen on pairs beyond the doubled wall?} No: reach beyond $d{=}18$ in the 24-node
component \emph{regresses}, from $\approx1.0$ to $\approx0.10$--$0.14$; nothing was learned
past the wall, something that was already there was lost. (4) \textbf{Was $K{=}2$ behaviour
preserved?} No. Cut stays perfect everywhere, but a new reach failure appears both at a small,
previously-irrelevant split ($a{=}2$) and gradually across $a{=}5$--$11$, where the checkpoint
used to be exactly $1.000$. (5) \textbf{Is the fix a real geometric change, or a pure threshold
shift?} Given everything but scale/bias is frozen, the only thing that can move \emph{is} the
threshold by construction --- but \S\ref{sec:res-fullgrid} shows this single shift has a much
broader effect than the two named cells: cut$(1,2)$ and cut$(1,3)$ improve across nearly the
whole $K{=}3$ grid, not just on the trained distribution, while cut$(2,3)$ improves only
partially and unevenly. That broad an effect from two scalars is itself informative: the
geometry the trunk already produces evidently places most $K{=}3$ cross-component pairs close
to the decision boundary, so a global bias/scale shift moves many of them at once --- in both
directions. \textbf{Net verdict for Experiment~1}: the targeted fine-tune is not a free
threshold correction. It trades reach (own-family and beyond-the-wall) for a partial, uneven
improvement in cut, and does not produce exact match on either target cell.

\section{Experiment 2: fine-tuning on the full $K{=}3$ split distribution}
\label{sec:exp2}

Experiment~1 asks whether the two-scalar fine-tune can fix two specific cells it is trained
directly on. It says nothing about whether that fix is a genuinely general three-component rule
or an overfit to those two cells' particular geometry. Experiment~2 asks the general-rule question
directly: fine-tune the same two scalars, from the same starting checkpoint, but on \emph{every}
three-way split instead of two, for a much longer budget.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup-full}

\paragraph{Checkpoint.} Identical starting point to Experiment~1 (\S\ref{sec:setup}): the
unmodified split-chains-only, $n{=}46$, seed-$1000$ checkpoint.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning.} Every parameter frozen except \texttt{sim\_scale}/\texttt{sim\_bias}, as
in Experiment~1. Training stream: \texttt{generate\_path\_union\_graph(n, rng, min\_paths{=}3,
max\_paths{=}3)} --- exactly three components every sample, with both cut points (hence every
component-size combination) drawn uniformly, freshly relabelled every sample --- instead of only
the two named cells. $200{,}000$ steps (4$\times$ Experiment~1's budget), batch $500$, AdamW lr
$10^{-3}$ (\texttt{finetune\_readout\_threeway\_full.py}).

\paragraph{What is measured.} The same per-cell metrics as Experiment~1 on the \emph{same} two
named cells $(15,15,16)$/$(7,15,24)$ (for a direct, apples-to-apples comparison against
Experiment~1's curves, Figures~\ref{fig:r10curve}--\ref{fig:r10beyond}), \emph{plus} an aggregate
exact-match/pairwise-accuracy number over freshly sampled, generic three-component graphs at every
evaluation (the same style of number Report~IX's own generic-$K{\ge}3$ fine-tuning probe
reported). The own-family $K{=}2$ sweep and the mechanistic battery (attention, five-stage
geometry) are reproduced exactly as in Experiment~1, into the \texttt{\_full3way}-tagged output
directories.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results-full}

\subsubsection{Does exposure to the full distribution generalise, where the targeted fine-tune
could only overfit two cells?}
\label{sec:res-full-aggregate}

Figure~\ref{fig:r10fullaggregate} gives the direct answer: no. Aggregate exact match over
freshly sampled, generic three-component graphs rises from $0.0$ at step $0$ to a peak of
$\approx0.05$ around step $15{,}000$--$20{,}000$, then settles back down to $\approx0.01$--$0.02$
for the remaining $180{,}000$ steps --- a handful of graphs out of $300$ per evaluation, not a
general rule. Pairwise accuracy rises more substantially, from $0.62$ to a plateau around
$0.74$, but pairwise accuracy is a much weaker bar than exact match (a graph with one wrong
pair out of hundreds still scores highly on it). Four times the steps and exposure to the
entire $K{=}3$ split space, instead of two named cells, does not turn read-out-only
fine-tuning into a general three-component rule: it confirms, at a longer budget and a purer
$K{=}3$ stream than Report~IX's own probe
(\texttt{scratch\_prof\_experiments.tex}, \S6, $3{,}000$ steps on a mixed $K\in\{3,4,5,6\}$
stream), that the representational gap sits in the frozen trunk, not in the read-out
threshold.

On the two named cells, the broader stream does slightly better on $(7,15,24)$'s cut$(2,3)$
($0.72$ at step $200{,}000$ vs.\ Experiment~1's $0.50$) but slightly \emph{worse} everywhere

both experiments. The pattern from \S\ref{sec:res-fullgrid} repeats at the level of the full
grid: cut$(1,2)$/cut$(1,3)$ again jump to $\approx1.0$ almost everywhere and cut$(2,3)$ again
follows the same jagged shape with a dip through $s_1{\approx}6$--$9$, but mean exact match
over the $176$ cells is $0.0174$ --- marginally higher than Experiment~1's $0.0100$, still
nowhere near a general rule. Longer, broader exposure does not change the qualitative story,
only nudges a few numbers in either direction.

\subsubsection{Own-family retention and scale/bias movement}
\label{sec:res-full-ownfamily}

\subsection{Reading, once the data is in}
\label{sec:verdict-b}

The aggregate $K{=}3$ exact match does not move meaningfully above $0$ ($0.013$ at
$200{,}000$ steps, after a small transient peak of $0.05$): read-out-only fine-tuning does not
produce a genuinely general three-component rule, no matter how much coverage or how long the
budget. This confirms, at much higher confidence than Report~IX's shorter, mixed-$K$ probe
(\S6 of \texttt{scratch\_prof\_experiments.tex}, $3{,}000$ steps), that the representational
gap is in the frozen trunk, not the read-out threshold. Comparing directly against
Experiment~1: the full-distribution run does not do uniformly worse or better on the two named
cells --- it trades a slightly better cut$(2,3)$ on $(7,15,24)$ for slightly worse reach
everywhere else, and a somewhat more damaged own-family sweep in the middle range. Spreading
two scalars' worth of capacity over the whole $K{=}3$ space does not cost the two hardest
cells much relative to the targeted run, because neither run was ever going to reach exact
match on them: both are re-arranging the same fixed, frozen geometry along the one axis
(scale/bias) available to them, and that axis is not expressive enough to fix more than a
fraction of the cross-component pairs at once.

\section{Combined verdict}
\label{sec:verdict}

\textbf{No amount of read-out-only fine-tuning tested here --- targeted on two cells for
$50$k steps, or exposed to the entire $K{=}3$ split space for $200$k steps --- gets a
three-component split-chains graph exactly right.} Both runs move the aggregate pairwise cuts
substantially (cut$(1,2)$ and cut$(1,3)$ go from noisy/moderate to $\approx1.0$ across nearly
the whole grid; cut$(2,3)$ improves unevenly), which on first glance looks like real progress
on \S5.3's question. But whole-graph exact match stays at $\approx0$--$0.02$ throughout,
because the same fine-tuning damages reach: on the very $K{=}2$ split-chains family the
checkpoint used to solve perfectly, exact match now degrades gradually from $a{=}5$ and fails
outright at a small split ($a{=}2$) that was never a problem before, and reach beyond the
doubled wall in the 24-node component of $(7,15,24)$ collapses from $\approx1$ to
$\approx0.03$--$0.14$. The two read-out scalars are expressive enough to move a decision
boundary that affects most pairs in the graph at once, but not expressive enough to separate
``pairs that need a stricter threshold'' (the failing cross-component cuts) from ``pairs that
need the old, looser threshold'' (long-range within-component reach) --- because both kinds of
pairs sit in the same region of the cosine-similarity geometry the frozen trunk already
produces. \textbf{For \S5.3: the fine-tuning experiments support describing the $K{\ge}3$
failure as a genuine representational gap in the trunk, not a miscalibrated read-out threshold
--- and additionally show that trying to patch the threshold anyway has a real cost, silently
breaking $K{=}2$ behaviour that was previously perfect.} This is consistent with, and now more
thoroughly evidenced than, Report~IX's own generic fine-tuning probe
(\texttt{scratch\_prof\_experiments.tex}, \S6, experiment 6), which reached the same
conclusion at a shorter budget and a narrower training stream.

are still not just noisier versions of each other now that both have more seeds: continuous
training gives close to even odds of landing a fully-solving seed at the hardest split
($4/10$, plus one seed --- $9000$ --- that gets there eventually via gradual reach recovery),
while grid training gives none ($0/10$ sustained solves; one seed, $5000$, reaches only a
stable partial plateau $\approx0.12$--$0.14$, never above it). This still suggests the seed
lottery on cycles interacts with \emph{how} the training distribution is built
(dense/continuous vs.\ sparse/grid), not only with initialization --- a comparison that was
not possible before, since \S2 of \texttt{scratch\_prof\_experiments.tex} only had 2 seeds for
the grid condition and reported seed disagreement without being able to say anything about the
continuous condition's own seed
count at the time.
